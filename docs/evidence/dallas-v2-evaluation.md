# Dallas V2 policy evaluation and performance evidence

The predeclared background-only baseline completed **90 of 90 runs**, with **zero failures**, all **30 complete paired seeds**, unchanged source hashes, and **32,490 external minute-boundary invariant checks**. Elapsed wall time was **38.88 minutes** with three concurrent Node processes. The serial benchmark completed **45 of 45 admitted trials**, with **16,245 external checks**, identical state/trace/summary digests across all fifteen trials at each size, and stable sources. Its **1,000-household processing target was missed**. The bounded sensitivity set completed **27 of 27 runs**, with zero failures, unchanged sources, and **9,747 external checks**, in **10.50 minutes** across three concurrent seed shards. These planned sets total **162 full-horizon runs and 58,482 external checks**; the separate default replay and CPU diagnostic are additional, explicitly labeled runs.

Coverage versus urgency first is a tradeoff, not an overall improvement: paired mean changes are **−11.67 people helped** (range −42 to +16), **−5.33 timely urgent needs** (−13 to +2), **+1,632.02 unmet-need minutes** (−359.26 to +3,787.02), **−20.83 unchecked homes** (−22 to −13), and **−0.23 undiscovered needs** (−2 to 0). Urgency first versus earlier updates first averages +21.07 timely urgent needs and −22 people helped. The preserved `received-order-v2` implementation orders by the household's **latest received report/check update**; a later update can reorder a known need. It is not a FIFO queue of first need receipts. The baseline reports that actual implementation without changing frozen engine behavior or replay. Raw paired differences and full simulation spread remain in `baseline-receipt.json`; independent household/person denominators and policy statistics are in `baseline-analysis.json`. These are results of authored assumptions, not measured disaster-response benefits.

Direct persona actors, eligible direct actors, and direct actions are **zero in every baseline run**. Actual suppression ranges **0–3**, because assistance can invalidate even background opportunities. Fallback actions range **397–561**, all with reason `no-prepared-artifact`. The actual per-run counters are preserved; no count is substituted from the separate prepared cohorts.

`node tools/v2/evaluate.mjs --workers=3` runs seeds **20261108–20261137** against exactly `received-order-v2`, `urgency-first-v2`, and `urgency-plus-coverage-v2`. Each seed uses one immutable population, map, facility configuration, need/event tape, support-demand allocation, resources, and exogenous background-behavior inputs across all three policies. The baseline is the admitted **3,061-household / 5,306-person** full-tract population, with **1,313 road nodes, 1,828 edges, and 68 restrictions**. Every run advances to minute 360 and asserts world invariants at initialization and each of 360 minute boundaries; the engine also checks its internal boundaries. This is an offline Node experiment, not browser interaction evidence.

The fixed prepared artifact is **empty (`null`)** in the entire paired experiment. Direct persona actors, eligible direct actors, direct actions, suppression, fallback actions, and reasons are retained per run. This background-only comparison does not claim coverage from separately generated cohorts. A genuine prepared-cohort case study at the reference seed is separate evidence.
